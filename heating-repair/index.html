No two heating systems break the same way, and in Scranton homes the first question on any no-heat call is what kind of equipment we are dealing with. Furnaces most often stall on an oxidized flame sensor. The burners fire, the sensor fails to confirm the flame, and after three attempts the control board closes the gas valve and locks the system out. Cracked hot surface igniters, sticking pressure switches, plugged condensate traps on high-efficiency units, and tired blower motors round out the usual list. Heat pumps have their own failure patterns. A defrost cycle that falls short lets ice build up on the outdoor coil, a bad reversing valve traps the system in cooling mode, and a dead outdoor unit hands the whole load to the electric backup strips, which cost several times as much to run.

Guessing gets expensive on a cold night, so Scranton HVAC handles every heating call as a diagnosis first and a repair second. Our techs measure before they replace anything: microamp flame signal, igniter resistance, pressure switch response to actual inducer draft, capacitor and motor readings, plus charge and defrost checks when the system is a heat pump. On gas equipment, safety checks are built in, not an add-on. Gas connections are leak-tested, the heat exchanger is inspected, and venting is verified, because warm air from an unsafe furnace is not a fix.

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Frequent on-off cycles that never bring rooms up to temperature
  • Clicking and failed ignition attempts before the furnace gives up
  • An outdoor heat pump coil that stays iced over

  • Booming or banging at ignition, a sign of delayed burner light-off

Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

Combustion is what makes heating repair different from the rest of home comfort work. Delayed ignition booms unburned gas inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal each time it happens. A cracked exchanger can leak combustion gases into the air your family breathes, and a flue that stops drafting pushes carbon monoxide into the living space instead of outdoors. None of these problems announce themselves to someone without training and instruments. Diagnosis has its own traps as well. Short cycling can point to a failing limit switch or to a furnace protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a fouled flame sensor without asking why simply sets up the next lockout.

That is why Scranton HVAC does not guess. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate so the blower and the fire are properly matched. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve rather than blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because that is what the machine heating your home deserves.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Make sure every level has a working carbon monoxide detector, and test it monthly
  • Change the filter monthly in heating season; starved airflow trips limit switches and cracks exchangers early
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Never store paints, solvents, or chlorine products near the furnace; their vapors corrode the burners
