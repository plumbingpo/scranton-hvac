Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Your last professional tune-up was more than twelve months ago
  • A surprise breakdown during the last hot or cold spell
  • Uncertainty about whether your system is in good shape or on borrowed time
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • A newer unit with warranty terms that call for regular professional service
  • Musty air when the fan starts, or a filter that has been in far too long
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • A startup sound that has changed since last year

Why Professional HVAC Maintenance Matters

Homeowners can take care of a short list: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and glancing at the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

Once gas is involved, maintenance becomes a safety issue. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they should never be, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. Scranton HVAC makes those safety checks the core of each fall visit, never an upsell. How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Get your tune-ups on the calendar during mild weather, well before the rush
  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Keep supply and return registers open in every room, even the ones you rarely use
  • Give the condenser room to breathe and rinse its coil lightly each spring
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
